Settings are saved automatically when your browser permits local-file storage; browsers can keep separate settings for different file paths or clear them with site data. Export/import JSON for a reliable backup. Changing the note count, key, scale, octave, pitch range, or maximum interval generates a new sequence when idle. With vowel “None”, the reference pass is skipped. In free play, the starting octave and pitch range determine the visible register (with an octave of margin above and below). Settings apply to the next round.

How to use: For sequence practice, click either play button and allow microphone permission. Listen as the marker crosses each bar, then sing along on the silent pass. Select vowel “None” to skip listening and sing straight away. In free play, pitch moves left past a fixed playhead; the trace uses the tuning meter’s colors to show how close you are to the nearest equal-tempered note (green within ±15 cents, yellow within ±30 cents, red beyond that). The meter’s ±50-cent endpoints are halfway between adjacent notes. Click “Stop free play” or press Esc to stop.

Pitch tracking: A simple YIN-style fundamental-frequency detector operates entirely in the page. It works best with a single voice, a reasonably quiet room, and headphones. Tracking has some latency and can make octave errors, particularly with breathy or noisy singing; the graph is feedback, not a medical or musical assessment.

Privacy: All synthesis and microphone analysis happen locally. The microphone is released when a sequence finishes, free play is stopped, or the tab is hidden. Only settings (never audio or pitch traces) are saved to local browser storage when available. The page makes no network requests and contains no third-party code.
